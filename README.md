**Physiology | Computational biology | Research software**

I'm a physiologist and software developer based in Winnipeg, Canada. I build tools for biological research, data analysis, and scientific learning, bringing experimental context to software design.

My MSc in Physiology & Pathophysiology at the University of Manitoba focused on [a 3D-bioprinted airway smooth muscle model for studying airway mechanics in asthma](https://mspace.lib.umanitoba.ca/items/f5b6aa9f-d968-4e65-a698-c0abaf1e048b).

As part of that work, I performed contraction assays using the Cytation 5, imaging bioprinted airways before and after administering contractile agents. Measuring their response meant manually tracing the lumen in each image and calculating the percentage reduction in its area. The process was tedious and prone to error. I began to wonder whether I could automate it.

That question led me to programming. From the moment I opened a terminal window, I knew there was no going back. I had started with a specific laboratory problem, but my interest quickly grew beyond solving it: I wanted to understand how software worked and what else I could build.

The idea grew into BioRing Analyzer, a browser-based tool for detecting bioprinted airway rings and measuring changes in lumen area. My interests expanded from automating an analysis to building complete applications, including the interfaces people interact with and the logic and data handling underneath. That progression led me into full-stack development.

I'm still interested in both sides of a research tool: the biological question it helps answer and the computational machinery that makes the answer possible.

## Selected projects

### [BioRing Analyzer](https://bioringanalyzer.netlify.app/)

A browser-based image-analysis tool for detecting bioprinted airway rings and measuring changes in lumen area. Connects computer vision with the experimental problem of quantifying airway contraction.

**Built with:** JavaScript, TensorFlow.js, OpenCV.js, D3.js.

### [Stroke Risk Prediction App](https://strokepredictor.netlify.app/)

An educational machine-learning application connecting an R modelling workflow to an interactive web interface through a Plumber API. Developed through AI4PH coursework on transparent and reproducible algorithms for public health.

**Built with:** R, tidymodels, Plumber, JavaScript, jQuery.

### [Bio-ink Calculator](https://alginatecalculator.netlify.app/)

A web calculator for bio-ink preparation and dilution, translating concentration and volume calculations into a reusable laboratory tool.

### [Computer Science in Java](https://github.com/egbonjefri/java_programming_robert_wayne)

Implementations and exercises from Robert Sedgewick and Kevin Wayne's *Computer Science: An Interdisciplinary Approach*. I use Java to explore programming, algorithms, data structures, and simulation by implementing the underlying ideas.

## Ongoing explorations

### First-principles science education

I'm developing biology and biochemistry learning materials that connect physical principles to biological function. The work combines structured lessons, mathematical derivations, interactive widgets, and questions that require reasoning across concepts.

The organizing principle is **structure -> mechanism -> consequence**: explain what a system is made of, how it behaves, and what follows from that behavior. Topics include protein folding, molecular binding, enzyme catalysis, and regulation.

### Computer architecture from logic gates

My TOY-8 design work explores how a small processor can be constructed from NOR logic, with explicit accounting for registers, arithmetic, multiplexers, memory, and control. I've also worked on a branch-and-link instruction extension, tracing how an instruction-set change propagates through the datapath.
